# Bombers' Secret Society of Justice

The Bombers are a group of children in Clock Town who describe their mission
as solving everyone's problems. Their leader is Jim. They first appear as a
membership test and later as a notebook that records optional problems,
deadlines and rewards.

## The code and hideout

During the opening sequence, Link can pop Jim's balloon with Deku magic and
accept a hide-and-seek challenge. Finding the other Bombers reveals the
secret code for the underground passage. The passage connects East Clock Town
to the Astral Observatory and is guarded by a Bomber.

After Link returns to human form, he can use the code, or repeat the test, to
join the group and receive the **Bombers' Notebook**. The original N64 and
Majora's Mask 3D versions present the notebook acquisition differently; a
retrieved document should preserve that version distinction.

## Hideout and early progression

The hideout is an access route, not merely a collectible room. It contains
platforming hazards and a small treasure. Completing the route opens access
to the observatory, where Link can continue the opening progression and learn
about the falling Moon.

## Notebook structure

The notebook groups optional tasks by person or problem. Entries provide hints
and often include a time window. Rewards can include masks, bottles, songs or
other items. Completing more entries can change the ending, so the notebook is
both a quest log and a planning aid.

Useful examples include Anju, Kafei, the Postman, Madame Aroma, the Bomb Shop
owner's mother and the Curiosity Shop owner. These are separate objectives
with separate triggers; a document hit on “Bombers” should not imply that all
notebook tasks are required for one goal.

## Planning notes

For a Bombers-related player request, identify whether the player wants:

- the hideout code;
- the notebook itself;
- one person's happiness entry;
- an optional reward; or
- the ending effect of notebook completion.

Only then should structured objectives and RAG evidence be combined. The
notebook is narrative assistance, while the MCP quest chain remains the source
of truth for this prototype's formal prerequisites.
